Explainer 10

Platform Capabilities

Explainers 01–09 cover the machinery: the chain, the proofs, the trust roots, the gates, the SSO. This one answers the question people actually arrive with: what can Jomhoor do? Every capability, in one place, with an honest status label on each.

Jomhoor is not a voting app with extras. It is the digital and procedural form of a common democratic field — a place where citizens can deliberate, vote, coordinate, and contribute to structured democratic processes at any scale, from a neighborhood consultation to a national dialogue.

One design commitment runs through everything below: privacy and democratic integrity are not competing values. They are co-equal design requirements, and the cryptography exists to make that true rather than aspirational.

The democratic sequence

Every process on Jomhoor follows the same recommended sequence. Deliberation always precedes decision — opinions are the input to democracy, not its output.

Voting and referenda

Binding and consultative votes with cryptographic integrity. Each verified human contributes exactly one vote, which makes large-scale synthetic interference structurally impossible rather than merely against the rules.

  • Single-choice and multi-choice votes
  • Ranked-choice and preferential voting
  • Referendum-style binary decisions
  • Anonymous but verified participation — ballot secrecy with eligibility proof
  • Auditable results without exposing individual choices

Structured deliberation

Deliberation is the core of the sequence, not a comment section bolted onto a poll.

  • Threaded discussion spaces organised by topic and scope
  • Opinion clustering: surfacing patterns of agreement across large groups
  • Polarisation reduction through consensus-discovery framing (inspired by the Pol.is model)
  • Subsidiarity enforcement: issues begin in the smallest relevant community before escalation
  • Collaborative policy review and iterative drafting
  • Multilingual deliberation support

Polling and public sensing

Measuring opinion honestly means publishing uncertainty instead of overclaiming reach.

  • Structured polling on policy questions, institutional options, and process design
  • Transparent, data-driven reporting on participation and outcomes
  • Attitudinal mapping across geographic and demographic segments
  • Integration with research-grade survey methodologies

Self-assessment and matchmaking

The Civic Compass side of the platform turns a concerned individual into a participating citizen.

  • Self-assessment against structured frameworks; democratic-literacy measurement
  • Skill-based and interest-based matchmaking between participants and initiatives
  • Team formation for civic projects, working groups, and task forces
  • Recruitment pathways linking individuals to organisations and roles

Collective action: DAOs and multi-sig

  • DAO governance modules: proposal submission, deliberation, and ratification flows
  • Role-based permissions and membership tiers within DAOs
  • Multi-signature wallets for collective asset control, with threshold approvals (e.g. 3-of-5) and DID-verified signatories

One wallet, one field: SSO

The Jomhoor Wallet is the identity and access layer for the whole ecosystem: single sign-on across platform features and partner portals, verifiable credential storage, and a participation history accessible only to its holder. Partner platforms receive proofs of uniqueness — never wallet addresses, never raw identity data. Details in explainer 09.

Organisational tools

Around the citizen-facing core sits an ecosystem for civil-society organisations and institutions: onboarding portals, profile management and verification, membership and internal-governance tools, and a modular ERP environment for coordination, training pathways, and knowledge management.

Hybrid participation

Democratic processes are neither one-off nor purely virtual: fully online participation from anywhere, hybrid events combining in-person and verified digital attendance, anonymous-but-verified mechanisms for high-risk contexts, and both synchronous and asynchronous deliberation modes.

The identity foundation, in one paragraph

Everything above sits on the identity layer covered in explainers 02 through 06: a self-sovereign DID generated on the user's device, backed by zero-knowledge proofs from government-issued documents. Noir and Circom circuits, audited by Halborn. All four biometric passport types relevant to the target population; multiple national ID card series; NFC where the document supports it, and an on-device ZKML liveness and face-likeness check (86% match threshold) where it does not. Because democratic accessibility and censorship resilience are inseparable, integration with circumvention tools such as Psiphon is part of the core implementation architecture — not an afterthought.

What the platform cannot do

Known limits we have to own.

  1. It cannot run a binding national referendum under unfree conditions. A real referendum requires universal suffrage, voter safety, free media, independent election authorities, and observation. Jomhoor is referendum-readiness infrastructure: it educates voters, tests ballot formats, runs transparent trial votes, and compares deliberative outcomes — so a genuine referendum can be executed the moment conditions allow.
  2. It cannot force adoption. We can build the field; we cannot make factions enter it.
  3. It cannot conjure connectivity. Inside a whitelisted, throttled internet, participation depends on circumvention. The stack is low-bandwidth by design and diaspora participation is unconstrained, but we do not pretend the domestic access problem is solved.

The press-kit-safe summary

If you are quoting Jomhoor to a journalist, an NGO, a UN agency, or a funder, this is what is true:

  • One verified human, one reusable identity, zero stored personal data. Trust comes from cryptographic proof, not data custody. This is not KYC.
  • Deliberation precedes decision. The platform's sequence is deliberate: discuss, cluster, refine — then vote.
  • The capability set is broad and mostly implemented: voting, deliberation, polling, self-assessment, matchmaking, DAO governance, multi-sig, SSO — with two items honestly labelled as prototyped or in development.
  • Equal standing is architectural. Entry routes differ; once inside, every participant has identical democratic standing, and the system cannot tell a famous exile from a provincial teacher.
  • The limits are named. No binding referenda under unfree conditions, no forced adoption, no magical connectivity.
